## 2. The narrative frame — 4 acts, 18 slides

You said you prefer storytelling. A technical FYP defence is not a features
list; it is one argument with evidence. The arc:

| Act | Slides | Job of the act | Emotion |
|-----|--------|-----------------|---------|
| **1. The person** | 1–4 | Make them feel the farmer's decision before you name your project | Concern |
| **2. The evidence** | 5–8 | Prove the problem is real, big, and unsolved | Certainty |
| **3. The machine** | 9–13 | Show exactly what you built and how it computes | Trust |
| **4. The proof** | 14–18 | Prove it is defensible, buildable, and honest | Respect |

**Timing for a 15-minute defence:** Act 1 ≈ 2 min, Act 2 ≈ 4 min, Act 3 ≈ 5 min,
Act 4 ≈ 4 min. Slides 9–13 get the most screen time because that is where the
FYP skill is actually demonstrated.

#### Slide 11 — The one calculation that decides the project (your single most important slide)

**On the slide** — the algorithm on the left, the validation on the right.

**LEFT — the method, as implemented:**

```
INPUT   Soil test card:  N, P, K (ppm or kg/ha), pH, soil type, acres
   │
1 │ CONVERT   1 acre-foot of soil = 1,000,000 kg  ⇒  1 ppm = 1 kg/acre
   │
2 │ RANK      Poor / Medium / Adequate against SFRI's band for this soil type
   │
3 │ SELECT    Crop requirement from the SFRI Guide-V table for that band
   │
4 │ SUBTRACT  nutrient needed = crop requirement − your soil test value
   │
5 │ CONVERT   kg nutrient → bags   (Urea 46% N  → 23 kg N per 50 kg bag)
                                (DAP  46% P₂O₅ → 23 kg P₂O₅ per bag)
                                (MOP  60% K₂O  → 30 kg K₂O per bag)
   │
6 │ CEIL      bags/acre × acres = total bags, rounded UP to whole bags
   │
   │   ⚠ zero bags returned when a nutrient is already above target
   │
OUTPUT  Urea bags · DAP bags · MOP bags · PKR cost · radar chart
```

## 4. "What is the REAL number for cost reduction?" — the honest answer

You asked for the real number. Here it is, and it is **not** a single number,
because no honest one exists. Anyone who gives you a single figure like "our app
saves Rs 8,000 per acre" has invented it. Give the panel **four levers, each
with its own citation and its own caveat**, and let the sum speak for itself.

### 4.1 The four levers

**Lever 1 — Do not buy fertiliser the soil does not need.**
- Fertiliser is **33.9% of total cost** of wheat per acre in Faisalabad
  (**Rs 23,184/acre**) — *Economic Analysis of Fertilizer Use*, 2024.
  Nationally, farmers spend **~Rs 1,200 billion a year** on fertiliser —
  *Pakistan Economic Survey 2025-26*.
- Your app returns **zero bags** when a nutrient is above target. In the
  reference screen, potassium is above the loam range → **0 bags of MOP**. At
  the potash price band, that is one bag of money the farmer does not spend.
- **Sensitivity to a price move:** NFDC states that **Rs 100 per 50 kg bag** of
  fertiliser is worth **Rs 20 billion a year** to the farming community
  nationally. Knowing your exact bag count is what lets you survive that move —
  it is the difference between a farmer who absorbs it and a farmer who cuts
  his dose and his yield.
- **Caveat to say out loud:** the Rs 100/bag → Rs 20bn figure is national, not
  per-acre. Do not present it as a per-acre saving.

**Lever 2 — Stop over-watering.**
- Central Punjab: farmers applied **430 mm** to wheat over 5 irrigations;
  bed-planted used **190 mm** — **56% less** — **PCRWR, 2023**.
- For rice: **302 cm** farmer practice vs **161 cm** bed-planted — **47% less**.
- Pakistan's crop water requirement: rice **500–1,500 mm**, wheat **353–562
  mm**; the same PCRWR review records farmers applying *"2–3 times or even
  more"* the actual requirement.
- Diesel pumping: **Rs 2,700 per irrigation round** at Rs 270/litre, **5 hrs/acre,
  2 L/hour** — Indian Express reporting Punjab farmer accounts, Feb 2023.
  **Recompute this before you use it:** diesel is now **Rs 395.85/litre**
  (HSD, effective 7 Oct 2026, Dawn), so the *same round* now costs roughly
  **Rs 3,960** — a **~47% increase**. A farmer irrigating 6 rounds on 6.2 acres
  spends **~Rs 1.47 lakh on pumping alone** at today's diesel.
- **This is your single most concrete rupee number.** Say it carefully:
  *"At today's diesel price, one wheat season of irrigation on six acres costs
  about one and a half lakh rupees in pumping alone. Our FAO-56 number tells
  him how many irrigations the crop actually needs and how much that water
  costs, before he turns on the pump."*
- **Caveat:** your app calculates the water *requirement*, it does not prescribe
  bed planting. Do not claim it saves 56% water. It saves the *unplanned
  rounds*, which the farmer currently has no way to know about.

**Lever 3 — Fix the imbalance, not just the level.**
- Only **14.28%** of Multan (Punjab) farmers used a balanced N:P ratio; those who
  did achieved roughly **8.5% higher wheat productivity** and a jump in technical
  efficiency from **70% to 84%** — Elahi et al., Punjab cotton-wheat study.
- Only **40.83%** of soil-test farmers actually applied the recommended doses;
  the most-cited reason (**69.01%**) was *"difficulty in understanding and
  following the recommended doses"* — Punjab soil-test adoption study.
- Following soil-health-card recommendations gave **paddy +3.70%** and
  **wheat +6.16%** yield **with lower fertiliser consumption** — same study.
- **This is the argument that your project is not a calculator.** A calculator
  tells you a number. Your app tells you the number *and* converts it into bags
  you can buy and rupees you can budget. The 69% figure is the proof that the
  missing step is not information, it is **translation**.

**Lever 4 — Buy the yield, not the input.**
- Wheat yield gap, Punjab: potential **6,825 kg/ha** vs actual **2,825 kg/ha** —
  a gap of **4,000 kg/ha** — ZTBL *Crop Yield Gap Analysis Pakistan*.
- APSIM modelling of the rice-wheat zone: farmers reach **48–56% of potential
  wheat yield**, and **nitrogen stress is the primary driver of the gap** — CSIRO,
  *Environmental Research Letters* (2019).
- Current wheat economics: production cost **Rs 3,304 per 40 kg** (Govt of
  Pakistan *Wheat Policy Analysis 2023-24*), support price **Rs 3,500 per 40 kg**
  (2025-26 procurement). **Margin per maund: Rs 196, about 5.9%.**

### 4.2 The number to actually say on a slide

Do not add these up on the slide — they are not independent and the sum would be
a fiction. Instead, state the shape:

> **Where the money leaks, per acre, in Punjab wheat:**
> - **Fertiliser: 33.9% of total cost** — and roughly two-thirds of the nitrogen
>   applied is surplus, because the ratio is **3.68 : 1** against a recommended
>   **2 : 1**.
> - **Water: farmers apply 2–3× the requirement.** The gap between 190 mm and
>   430 mm on wheat is the single largest avoidable line item, and at today's
>   diesel price pumping costs roughly **Rs 4,000 per irrigation round**.
> - **Margin for error: Rs 196 per maund** between cost of production and support
>   price. That is **5.9%**. One wrong fertiliser bag erases it.
>
> **AgriPulse does not claim a rupee saving. It claims the number that every one
> of those savings depends on — and it is the only tool that produces that
> number from two inputs the farmer already has, offline, with the source
> printed beside it.**

### 4.3 The cost-reduction argument your teacher will accept

If you are pushed to produce *one* number, say this instead:

> "The farmer does not save money by using AgriPulse; he saves money by
> **stopping the wrong purchase**. Our reference case is 6.2 acres of wheat.
> Fertiliser is 33.9% of the Rs 23,184 per-acre cost — about **Rs 7,860**. If
> a soil test shows potassium is already above the target range for loam soil,
> the correct dose is **zero MOP bags**, and zero bags is money the farmer keeps.
> Our system can tell him that, in rupees, before he buys — and it can tell him
> the same for nitrogen and phosphorus. That is the entire economic claim: we
> convert a recommendation sheet into a purchase decision."
