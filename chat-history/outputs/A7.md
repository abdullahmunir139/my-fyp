# SECTION 1 — AIM AND OBJECTIVE (paste directly)

## 1.1 Aim

To build **AgriPulse**, an offline web application that turns a soil test card
and a land size into the four decisions a farmer must make before sowing —
**cost, water, fertiliser bags and profit** — in Pakistani Rupees and litres,
with every number showing the government or FAO publication it came from, so
that the farmer can decide without paying anyone, without an internet
connection, and without a subscription.

## 1.2 Objectives

Each objective is written so that it can be **marked pass or fail**.

**O1.** The system shall read a soil test card and show the farmer what his soil
is short of, using a radar chart that plots his value against the target range
for his soil type.

**O2.** The system shall calculate the **exact** number of Urea, DAP and MOP bags
using the **Government of the Punjab SFRI Guide-V formula** — not an invented
formula — and the result must land on **2.35 bags Urea and 1.04 bags DAP per
acre** for cotton, the figure published in the PCPA Cotton Book.

**O3.** The system shall calculate the total cost of sowing, broken down line by
line into land preparation, seed, fertiliser, labour, water and transport, with
every line matching the PCPA Cotton Book or the PAU Package of Practices figure
for that crop.

**O4.** The system shall calculate the total water need in litres and the number
of irrigations using the **FAO-56** method, with crop coefficients cited from
FAO-56 Table 12.

**O5.** The system shall show the expected yield, net profit, ROI and the
break-even price, using the PAU district yield band for yield and NFDC for
price.

**O6.** The system shall let the farmer move sliders and see cost, profit and ROI
change **immediately**, so that he can compare crops before committing, with the
recompute happening **in the browser and with zero network calls**.

**O7.** The system shall work **fully offline**, so that field signal is never a
problem — every screen still opens and every calculation still runs with the
router off.

**O8.** The system shall show the **source** of every number on the screen, next
to the figure rather than hidden behind a help icon, so that both the farmer and
the supervisor can trust it.

**O9.** The system shall keep the farmer's data private — one farmer can never
see another's — using JWT login where every protected query is filtered by the
logged-in user's ID.

## 1.3 The one idea the farmer should learn

The system should teach one thing that no calculator can teach: **more land with
the same budget raises total profit but lowers the return on each rupee.** That
single observation is the intellectual content of this project.

# SECTION 2 — SCOPE OF THE PROJECT (paste directly)

## 2.1 The scope, in one paragraph

The project delivers a **five-page offline web application** built from **four
functional modules**, served by **six API route files** and **nine backend
packages**. The farmer registers once, records his district, land area and soil
type, enters the N-P-K numbers from his soil test card, and receives the exact
fertiliser bags, the full cost breakdown, the water requirement in litres, the
expected yield, the net profit, the ROI and the break-even price — each figure
carrying the name of its public source. A what-if simulator then lets him move
sliders on area, budget and crop and watch cost, profit and ROI change live, so
that two crops can be compared side by side before money is committed. The
entire application runs on one laptop with the internet switched off, and it
holds **no machine-learning model of any kind**.

## 2.2 In scope — what the project WILL build

- **M0 — Login and Register.** Email and password with JWT, one account per
  farmer. *(Page 1)*
- **M1 — Land and Climate Profiler.** District, area in acres, soil type, and
  the climate card for that district and month. *(Pages 2 and 3)*
- **M2 — Resource and Financial Planner.** Full cost breakdown, water in
  litres, yield, net profit, ROI and break-even price. *(Page 4A)*
- **M3 — NPK Soil Fertiliser Advisor.** Deficiency ranking, exact Urea, DAP and
  MOP bag counts, fertiliser cost, and the radar chart. *(Page 5)*
- **M4 — What-If Simulator.** Sliders for area, budget and crop, live recompute,
  and reset to baseline. *(Page 4B)*

**Coverage and data volume:**

- **5 crops** — wheat, rice, maize, potato, tomato (the five with both agronomy
  and disease data available for Punjab).
- **5 soil types** — sandy, sandy loam, loam, clay loam, clay.
- **30 districts** of Punjab and Sindh, from the provincial Agriculture
  Departments, with **360 climate records** (30 districts × 12 months).
- **420 total reference rows**, loaded by one seed script.
- **3 user collections** — user, land, plan. **0 model files in the repository.**

## 2.3 Out of scope — what the project will NOT build, and why

This list is written down **on purpose**. A project that says no to things is a
project that gets finished.

- **Leaf disease identification from a photo.** It needs a trained model. We have
  no GPU, no labelled dataset and no server, and the existing free models cover
  38 US horticulture classes, not Punjab's crops. **Deferred to future work.**
- **Mandi (market) price feed.** It needs a paid live feed and a server, which
  would turn the project into a market app.
- **Live weather API.** It needs the internet, and the offline promise is the
  core of the project.
- **IoT sensors, weather stations and satellite imagery.** Hardware is the cost
  we are removing, not adding.
- **Saved plans, history and PDF reports.** These would double the work. Plans
  last for the current session only, and the app states this on screen instead of
  hiding it.
- **Urdu voice audio, chat and notifications.** Text only — not enough time, and
  not what the problem statement needs first.
- **A native mobile app.** The web app installs on a phone as a Progressive Web
  App; a second codebase is not needed.
- **Marketplace, job board and livestock records.** These belong to a different
  product.
- **Multi-farmer or organisation accounts, roles and an admin panel.** One
  farmer, one account.
- **Cotton, sugarcane and seven other crops.** Five crops only, as listed above.

## 2.5 The seven-week plan, with the gate for each week

- **Week 1** — Build the four data files and the seed script. *Gate: 420 rows are
  in MongoDB.*
- **Week 2** — Express server, JWT login, six routes, Zod validation. *Gate:
  `POST /api/auth/login` returns a token.*
- **Week 3** — Screens 1–3 and the climate service. *Gate: district and N-P-K
  save; the climate card shows.*
- **Week 4** — Screen 4 Tab A and the planner service. *Gate: cost, water, yield
  and ROI appear with citations.*
- **Week 5** — **Screen 5, the SFRI service and the radar chart.** *Gate: the bag
  counts match the PCPA published cotton figures.*
- **Week 6** — Screen 4 Tab B, simulator, responsive layout, dark theme. *Gate:
  sliders recompute live and the mobile layout holds.*
- **Week 7** — Unit tests on four services, offline PWA, final report. *Gate:
  `npm test` is green and the app works with the router off.*

**Week 5 is the project. Every other week is ordinary engineering.**

## 2.6 Risks and the response to each

- **Scope grows during the build** (high / high) — the out-of-scope list is
  signed off; nothing is added without removing something.
- **The formula does not match published figures** (medium / **very high**) —
  week 5 is reserved for exactly this test; if it fails, the formula is fixed
  before anything else.
- **Reference data cannot be found** (medium / high) — every figure has a named,
  downloadable public source; the fallback is NFDC plus provincial department
  rates.
- **The supervisor asks for the removed disease module** (high / medium) — it is
  already written down as future work, with the reason. We do not pretend.
- **Offline mode breaks on refresh** (medium / medium) — the service worker is
  tested in week 7 with the router physically switched off.
