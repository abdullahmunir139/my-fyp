# c001 — Proposal for AgriPulse (Soil-Test Driven Farm Planner)

## What was done

Wrote the complete FYP proposal for **AgriPulse**, an offline web application that
turns a farmer's soil test card and land size into four pre-sowing decisions —
total cost in PKR, water need in litres, exact Urea/DAP/MOP bag counts, and
expected profit with ROI. The proposal was built directly inside the official
college template (`chat-history/media/1-FYP Proposal Template.docx`) and follows
its section order exactly: institution header, date and project code, project
title, tools to be used, problem statement, aim and objective, scope of the
project, functional requirements, non-functional requirements, particulars of
the students, and remarks with the signature block.

Content highlights:

- **Problem statement** grounded in an audit of 13 existing tools (5 Pakistani,
  8 international) across 4 problem areas and 16 factors, with the defensible
  gap stated in one line.
- **9 numbered objectives**, each written with a pass/fail proof, including
  validation of the central SFRI Guide-V algorithm against the published PCPA
  Cotton Book figure of 2.35 bags Urea + 1.04 bags DAP per acre.
- **Scope discipline**: 10 explicitly out-of-scope items each with a stated
  reason, plus an honest coverage scorecard (11 of 16 factors fully solved,
  2 partial, 3 deferred to future work).
- **51 functional requirements** in 6 groups and **20 non-functional
  requirements** in 8 categories, every one carrying a test that can fail.
- **Annexure A** listing the 7 public government and FAO data sources, and
  **Annexure B** listing 6 things the system explicitly does not claim.
- **27 tools** listed, each with the reason it was chosen.

Four specialised agent skillsets were applied read-only to shape the document:
proposal strategy (win themes, evidence per claim, no empty adjectives), plain
technical writing (short sentences, progressive disclosure), project management
discipline (exact requirements, realistic scope, testable acceptance criteria),
and software architecture (technology choices with stated reasons).
