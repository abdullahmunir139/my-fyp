## 3. The defensive core — what now makes it hard to reject

Three things carry the weight now, and they reinforce each other:

**1. The Evidence table.** Every number is one of three kinds — published
figure, calculated, or gated target — and never two at once. This single device
kills the "you made that up" attack, because it is visible before any
individual number is even read.

**2. The crop-sourcing rule.** This is the answer to your data problem. Rather
than claiming five crops and hoping, the proposal commits to a rule that makes
the honest outcome acceptable: a crop that is not sourced is disabled in the
interface, the seed script reports it, and shipping four crops instead of five
is a planned outcome, not a failure. An examiner cannot call that overclaiming
because the proposal never overclaimed.

**3. The Deployment and Use section.** You asked whether this is a strong point
or a weak one. Honest answer: as it stood, it was the single most dangerous
line in the document, because the proposal claimed a farmer would use an app
that runs on a laptop in a college lab. Written out plainly — what ships, how
a farmer reaches it, and what is not being claimed — it becomes one of the
strongest parts, because a reviewer who raises it finds it already answered,
in writing, with the limits named. Rejecting a proposal for honesty about its
own limits is not a move a supervisor wants to make.
